Cliff & Marginal Relief Architecture

Never Pay ₹25,000 Tax for Earning ₹5,000 Extra

Under the New Tax Regime, taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 enjoys a full Section 87A rebate (zero tax). But what happens if you earn ₹7,05,000? ITR-TaxPilot’s mathematical engine enforces Section 87A Marginal Relief.

How Section 87A Marginal Relief Works:

Without marginal relief, a taxpayer earning ₹7,05,000 would pay ₹25,500 in tax—meaning ₹5,000 extra salary costs ₹25,500 in taxes (an effective marginal tax rate of over 500%!).

Statutory Marginal Relief Formula: Tax Payable = Minimum(Computed Tax, Taxable Income - ₹7,00,000) + 4% Cess

For ₹7,05,000 income, the excess over ₹7,00,000 is ₹5,000. Marginal relief ensures your tax before cess is capped strictly at ₹5,000 instead of ₹25,500!

Filing Form Decision Tree

Which ITR Form Should You File?

Selecting the wrong ITR form is the #1 reason for defective return notices u/s 139(9). ITR-TaxPilot automatically detects your income sources to guide you to the exact form.

ITR-1 (Sahaj)

For Salaried Residents with Simple Income

  • Total Income up to ₹50,00,000
  • Income from One Salary and One House Property
  • Other sources income (Interest, Dividends)
  • Agricultural income up to ₹5,000
  • Not eligible if capital gains, crypto, or directorship exists
ITR-2

For Individuals & HUFs with Capital Gains

  • Total Income exceeds ₹50 Lakh
  • Equity / Mutual Fund Capital Gains (111A, 112A)
  • Multiple House Properties & Loss Set-Off
  • Foreign Assets & Foreign Income Schedule
  • Not for Business or Professional income
ITR-3 & ITR-4 (Sugam)

For Business, Freelancers & Professionals

  • Presumptive Taxation u/s 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE (ITR-4)
  • F&O, Intraday trading, or Business Profits (ITR-3)
  • Professionals (Doctors, Tech Freelancers, Lawyers)
